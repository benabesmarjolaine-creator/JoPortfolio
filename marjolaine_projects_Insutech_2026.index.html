Process Automation – Risk Reduction – Provider Network
Turning invoice management into a GED-driven operating model
Antaé · Montreux · 2016–2019
DMAIC GED / EDM TPA Model
Context
During a meeting about a three-year contract renewal, we met with the client's decision makers, finance leaders, and operational teams, including student affairs and nurses. The client was satisfied with the insurance and medical assistance solution itself, including the insurer, premium, and service offer.

The concern came from the operational teams, who flagged issues with medical invoices being sent directly to students or the school on campus. Unlike a local Swiss insurer, the international model did not consistently support direct billing for outpatient care, and students often had to pay providers upfront because the insurer was not always recognized. The problem was not whether the invoices would be paid, but how they were received and handled within the operational flow between provider, student, and insurer.

In practice, the existing glocal model was not performing well enough operationally.

Data Architecture – Mapping – Automation
Provider network upload standardization
April International Care France · Paris · 2020–2022
Data Mapping Data Governance TPA Network Integration Patient Steering
Context
A CSM system and client-facing app were already in place to help members find in-network providers, but provider data updates remained manual. Although bulk upload automation was already on the IT roadmap, a VIP tender with a short deadline accelerated the project so the full medical network could be presented to the client. The goal was to translate business requirements into a structured process for cleaning, mapping, and uploading provider network data.
Challenges
Uncharted territory
  • The bulk upload had never been attempted before
  • Uncertainty about whether it would work and whether the CSM system could support such a high volume of providers
  • The file format had to be defined, created, and tested from the ground up
Data quality & fragmentation
  • Provider data came from multiple sources in different formats
  • Needed to be cleaned, standardised, and made usable in both backend and app
  • Duplicates, incomplete records, and ghost providers to remove
Approach — 7 phases
Outcome
VIP tender supported
Fully operational provider network in the app, enabling the client to see the network in real time.
Bulk upload live
5,000+ providers integrated, improving data quality and usability across backend and member app.
Repeatable framework
Standardised, documented process replicable by future teams without starting from scratch.
Patient steering enabled
Preferred network steers members toward conventions and negotiated discounts, improving cost containment.
Key insight
The real challenge was not the upload itself, but designing the rules, structure, and governance needed for the network to work in both the backend and the app.

Context
For a tailored school solution, our team took over the full contract lifecycle, including applications to the insurance. Since students were still applying through paper forms during on-site visits, we designed and implemented a fully digital membership journey to improve user experience and replace the manual process within a short delivery window before campus arrival.
Challenges
Tight deadlines
The project had tight deadlines and was based on a similar model already in place, but not an exact replica.
Journey design balance
Designing a customer journey that balanced operational efficiency and error prevention without making the application process too long or difficult to complete.
Approach — 7 steps
End-to-end enrolment flow (BPMN)
Outcome

Cross-Functional – Process Mapping – US Market – Operational Alignment
Project Simply America — US Corridor Operational Model
April International Care France – Paris – 2022–2024
Cross-functional Leadership Process Mapping US TPA Management Case Management High-Cost Claims Governance
Context — the most complex market in the portfolio
The US corridor was the most operationally complex and financially sensitive part of the portfolio.

The American healthcare system — with its PPO networks, CVS/Caremark set up, CPT and DRG billing codes, and in-network/out-of-network cost differentials — operates differently from IPMI markets we might be more used to.

Paris, Mexico, and the US TPA were working in silos, which created mistrust, confusion over responsibilities, gaps in service quality, inconsistent handling of high-cost cases and poor coordination across the customer and claims lifecycle.
Challenge
Sales & Onboarding
  • Product not always understood and explained correctly to the client
  • No standardized onboarding process for new clients
  • Setup delays reduced onboarding efficiency and delayed access to care, direct billing, and client satisfaction
Teams Coordination
  • Key operational flows not clearly owned or understood
  • Lack of a shared view across teams
  • Mistrust between teams affecting day-to-day collaboration
Case & Claims Handling
  • Pharmacy card and PPO network not well integrated in practice
  • Open invoices not consistently reconciled between entities
  • No clear escalation path for complex or high-cost cases
Cost & Resilience
  • High-cost claims not subject to a structured review process
  • BCP mapping incomplete — operational resilience at risk
  • Members not systematically steered toward lower-cost care options
Approach — two years, three teams, end-to-end
Outcomes
Operational dialogue restored
Paris, Mexico, and the US TPA moved from siloed working to a shared follow-the-sun model.
Clearer US operating model
Documented workflows and responsibilities helped each team work more effectively.
Better case handling
Improved coordination reduced case management gaps and improved member service consistency.
High-cost claims governance
Structured review process aligned with insurer regulations for the most financially exposed claims.
Stronger operational resilience
BCP mapping and critical flows updated, database maintained, reducing operational risk.
Improved care navigation
Member communications and team training improved guidance on coverage and the US healthcare system.
Key insight
The portfolio's biggest challenge was not the US market itself, but the lack of a shared operating model around it. Once roles, flows, data, governance, training, and member guidance were aligned, service quality and cost control improved together.
